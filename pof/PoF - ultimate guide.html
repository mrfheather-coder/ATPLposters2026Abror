01 Controls & Surfaces
Aileron Reversal

At high speed, upgoing aileron creates a twisting moment on the flexible outer wing. The wing twists nose-down, reducing AoA and lift on that wing — opposite to intended roll.

At aileron reversal speed, twist effect exactly cancels aileron input. Beyond it, you roll the wrong way.

Solution

Use inboard ailerons at high speed (stiffer wing section) + spoilers for roll. Outboard ailerons locked out at high speed.

Spoiler Advantages

Spoilers deployed asymmetrically for roll control at high speed. Three key advantages from B767 study:

1. No flutter problems
2. Leave trailing edge free for flaps
3. No twisting moment on the wing

Spoilers reduce lift by destroying upper-surface suction. They decrease the critical AoA.

THS vs Trim Tab

THS — entire horizontal stabiliser pivots. Primary trim on airliners. Handles big, slow, persistent changes (fuel burn, speed). Elevator returns to neutral after trimming.

Trim tab — small tab on elevator trailing edge. More common on GA aircraft. Repositions elevator mechanically — control column moves to new neutral.

Fully powered hydraulics + THS: control column always returns to centre after trimming. Trim tab system: control column neutral position moves.
Fuel Transfer to Tail Trim Tank

Forward CG → large nose-down moment → THS provides big downward force → wings must overcompensate → more trim drag.

Solution: pump fuel aft to tail trim tank → CG moves rearward → THS works less → less trim drag. Carefully managed by flight computers to balance efficiency vs stability.

Remember
Forward CG = Stable but Draggy
Wing Layout — B767 Reference

Inboard flap (near root) — Fowler type, big lift + area increase. Outboard flap — further along span. Inboard aileron (flaperon) — between flap sections, used at high speed. Outboard aileron — at tip, locked out at high speed. Spoilers — on upper surface, used for roll + speedbrakes. Slats — leading edge, full span.

02 Pressure Distribution & Stall
Pressure Distribution vs AoA

Upper surface pressure profile: High (stagnation) → Low (suction peak) → High (trailing edge).

As AoA increases:

→ Suction peak gets stronger and moves forward
→ Adverse pressure gradient becomes longer and steeper
→ Boundary layer separates earlier
→ At critical AoA: flow fully separates = stall

Spoiler Effect on Critical AoA

Spoiler creates a sudden steep pressure rise (wall of adverse gradient). Boundary layer separates at the spoiler location — much earlier than on a clean wing.

Since flow is already partially separated at low AoA, it takes a smaller AoA to fully stall → critical AoA decreases.

Spoiler steals the boundary layer's energy budget early. Less margin left for increasing AoA.
Pressure Gradient Summary
Favourable Gradient

Pressure falling (accelerating flow). Leading edge → suction peak. Flow stays attached easily.

Adverse Gradient

Pressure rising (decelerating flow). Suction peak → trailing edge. Boundary layer fights to stay attached. Gets steeper with higher AoA.

Equilibrium

All forces balanced: L = W, T = D. Aircraft is NOT stationary — it's flying at constant velocity (unaccelerated flight). Newton's 1st law: no net force = no acceleration, not no motion.

Aerodynamic Centre vs CP

CP — moves with AoA (forward as AoA increases). Nightmare for stability calculations.

AC — pitching moment stays constant regardless of AoA. Fixed at ~25% chord (subsonic), ~50% chord (supersonic). Used for all stability analysis.

As AoA ↑: Lift ↑ but arm (AC to CP) ↓ proportionally → L × d = M stays constant.

Absolute Angle of Attack

Measured from the zero lift line (not chord line). At αa = 0°, lift = 0. The CL vs αa graph passes through the origin for ALL aerofoils regardless of camber. Used for stability analysis because it standardises the reference.

For a positively cambered wing: αa > α (zero lift line is above chord line).

Thrust Line & Pitching Moments

Propeller ahead of CG + aircraft inclined to airstream → thrust line above CG → pitch-up moment → destabilising (reinforces the disturbance).

Engines below CG Increase thrust → nose-up pitch. Engines above CG Increase thrust → nose-down pitch. Engines at CG No pitching moment — worst case for throttle-only control.

05 Stability
Static vs Dynamic Stability

Static — initial tendency after disturbance. Does it try to return?

Dynamic — what happens over time. Do oscillations die out?

Static stability is a PREREQUISITE for dynamic stability. No static stability = no oscillation = dynamic stability is meaningless.
StaticDynamicBehaviour
PositivePositiveReturns, oscillations die out
PositiveNeutralOscillates forever, constant amplitude
PositiveNegativeOscillations grow — divergent
Neutral—Stays wherever displaced
Negative—Diverges immediately. No oscillation.
CG & Longitudinal Stability

CG must be ahead of AC for positive longitudinal static stability.

Forward CG: longer arm to tail → stronger restoring moment → more stable, but more trim drag, higher stick forces.

Aft CG: shorter arm to tail → weaker restoring moment → less stable, but more efficient (less trim drag).

Aft CG limit: the point beyond which the tail cannot produce sufficient restoring moment. Beyond this → unstable.

The tailplane works across the entire certified CG range. It's just more or less effective depending on CG position.
Slip vs Skid
Slip

Slip = Same direction as turn (inward). Too little bank or rudder. Aircraft slides toward centre of turn.

Skid

Skid = opposite direction (outward). Like a car skidding wide on a corner. Too much bank or rudder.

06 Propellers
Pitch Concepts

Geometric Pitch — distance blade WOULD travel in one rev (design, theoretical).

Effective Pitch — distance blade ACTUALLY travels (always less).

Slip — the difference: Geometric − Effective.

Static on ground (brakes on, full power): slip is MAXIMUM. Effective pitch = 0.
Blade Twist

Blade angle is largest at root, smallest at tip. Why? The tip moves faster rotationally. Without twist, tip AoA would be too high (stalled) while root barely works.

Twist (washout) gives each section approximately the same AoA → even loading along the blade.

Propeller Efficiency
η = THP / SHP
Usable (thrust) power ÷ Shaft (brake) power

SHP (Shaft Power) = input from engine to prop shaft.
THP (Thrust Power) = output — what actually produces thrust.

Typical peak efficiency: 80–88%

Constant Speed Prop Behaviour

Governor maintains constant RPM by adjusting blade angle.

IAS increases (e.g. takeoff roll)

Blade AoA tends to drop → RPM tends to rise → governor increases blade angle (coarsens) → AoA restored → RPM constant.

Blade angle: VARIES (increases). Blade AoA: STAYS CONSTANT. RPM: STAYS CONSTANT.
Twisting Moments
MomentDirectionCause
CTM (Centrifugal)→ Fine pitchMass offset from pitch axis + rotation
ATM (Aerodynamic)→ Coarse pitchCP offset from pitch axis + aero force
Fine pitch stop exists because CTM would drive blade fully flat if governor fails.
Torque at Constant RPM

Constant RPM = no angular acceleration = engine torque = propeller torque.

But SHP > THP — losses appear in power, not torque. Same torque, but some energy wasted as heat/slipstream swirl.

Don't confuse torque balance (equal at constant RPM) with power efficiency (SHP > THP always).
Thrust Distribution

Root: low (slow, small sections). Mid-span (~70-75%): peak thrust. Tip: drops to zero (tip vortices + compressibility).

Maximum thrust is NOT at the tip — it peaks around 70-75% span then drops off.
Solidity & Blade Count
Solidity = (Blades × blade area) / disc area

More blades → higher solidity → can absorb more power. Used when diameter is limited (tip speed constraints on twin engines).

Gyroscopic Precession — Clockwise Prop (Pilot's View)

Rule: force applied to disc → reaction appears 90° ahead in direction of rotation. The force location tells you where it acts; the direction of force there gives you the resulting motion.

For anti-clockwise prop: reverse all results. Go-around (pitch up) with clockwise prop → yaw RIGHT.
Counter vs Contra-Rotating

Counter-rotating — two props on separate engines (one each wing) spinning opposite directions. Torques cancel. No critical engine.

Contra-rotating — two props on the SAME shaft, one behind the other, spinning opposite. Also cancels torque.

Torque Reaction

Always acts opposite to propeller rotation (Newton's 3rd law). Counter-rotating props: torques oppose each other → stabilising (they cancel out).

Limit vs Ultimate Load

Limit load factor — certification number. Fixed by design category. Does NOT change with aircraft weight. It's a ratio (n = L/W).

Limit load (force) — DOES change with weight. Limit load = n × W. Heavier aircraft = higher actual force at same n.

Ultimate load = 1.5 × limit load. No structural failure, but permanent deformation allowed.

Lighter aircraft at same n: lower actual force on structure → more structural margin. But the limit load FACTOR doesn't change.

09 Engine Failure & Asymmetric Flight
OEI — Flap Effect on Roll

Engine failure during go-around with flaps extended:

Live engine wing: slipstream energises flaps → MORE lift.
Dead engine wing: no slipstream → flaps in calm air → LESS lift.

Flaps amplify the asymmetric lift — they're more sensitive to slipstream energy. More flap = bigger lift difference = bigger roll toward dead engine.

Flaps INCREASE the roll. That's why reducing flap is part of the engine failure procedure.
Sideslip & Speed — Rudder Authority

As speed decreases with one engine out:

1. Asymmetric thrust moment stays roughly constant
2. Rudder effectiveness drops (less dynamic pressure)
3. Rudder can't fully oppose yaw → sideslip increases
4. Airflow hits fin at greater angle → rudder even less effective
5. Vicious cycle until Vmca

VMCA = minimum speed for directional control with OEI. Below this, rudder simply can't cope.
Wings-Level vs Banking Method

Wings-level: rudder only, aircraft flies with sideslip. More drag but simpler.

Banking (~5° toward live engine): reduces sideslip, less drag, more efficient. BUT relies on lateral component of weight (W sin φ) to help balance forces.

Light aircraft mass = less lateral weight component = less help balancing asymmetric thrust = harder with banking method. Problems at LIGHT masses, not heavy.
Throttle-Only Control

With total hydraulic failure, thrust can control pitch (engines above/below CG) and yaw (differential thrust on multi-engine).

Extremely difficult — throttle response is slow (seconds vs instant for control surfaces). Results in phugoid oscillations.

Case studies: UA 232 (crash-landed, 185/279 survived), JAL 123 (total loss), DHL Baghdad (all crew survived).
