A

ANVIL TOP

Top of CB, above freezing level
  • ⚡ Moderate to severe turbulence
  • 🧊 Hail likely (thrown up by updraft)
  • ❄️ NO significant icing
Why no icing? At this altitude, all moisture exists as ice crystals, not supercooled water droplets. Ice crystals bounce off the airframe rather than adhering and accreting.
B

DOWNWIND / UNDER ANVIL

Outside cloud, under anvil overhang
  • 🧊 HAIL in clear air!
  • 💨 Clear Air Turbulence
Sneaky danger! Hail gets thrown into anvil by updraft, carried downwind, then falls out into clear sky. You can be in VMC, blue sky above, and get hammered by hail you never saw coming. The "anvil's shadow" zone.
C

UPWIND EXTERIOR

Outside cloud, upwind of storm
  • 💨 Some CAT from proximity
  • ⚡ Generally mildest zone
Why relatively safe? Storm moving left → C is upwind (right side). The storm hasn't "polluted" this air yet. No hail (thrown downwind to B), no icing (outside cloud). Best approach route if you must go near.
D

MID-CLOUD INTERIOR

Inside CB, middle section
  • 🧊 Icing likely
  • ⚡ Moderate to severe turbulence
  • ⚡ Lightning expected
  • 🌧️ Heavy precipitation, hail
Inside the beast: You're in the active convective zone where updrafts and downdrafts battle. Supercooled water + turbulence + lightning = avoid at all costs.
E

WORST ZONE - ABOVE 0°C ISOTHERM

Inside CB, just above freezing level
  • 🧊 SEVERE ICING - Maximum hazard!
  • ⚡ Severe turbulence
  • ⚡ Lightning - majority here
  • 🌧️ Heavy precip, hail
The "sweet spot" for icing: Just above 0°C with maximum supercooled liquid water content being carried up by powerful updrafts. This is where icing is most severe and rapid. AVOID!
F

GUST FRONT

Ahead of storm at surface
  • 💨 GUST FRONT - sudden wind shift
  • 💨 Severe windshear
  • ⚠️ Extremely dangerous for takeoff/landing
Leading edge of chaos: Cold downdraft air races out AHEAD of storm movement along surface. Like a snowplow pushing air forward. Causes sudden, violent wind shifts. The storm is moving left, so F gets hit first.
G

TRAILING OUTFLOW

Behind storm at surface
  • 💨 Some turbulence
  • 💨 Mild windshear (less than F)
  • 🌧️ Trailing precipitation possible
Not the gust front! Downdraft spreads both ways when hitting ground, but forward rush (F) is stronger due to storm's own momentum. G gets "disturbed leftovers" - still turbulent but less violent than F.
H

MICROBURST ZONE

Below cloud, under precipitation
  • 💨 MICROBURSTS - extreme danger!
  • 💨 Intense windshear
  • 🌧️ Heavy precipitation
  • ⚠️ Fatal for approach/departure
Downdraft impact zone: Heavy rain drags air down. When this column hits ground, it spreads out violently in all directions. Aircraft on approach can lose 50+ knots airspeed in seconds. Many accidents occur here.
