CVOR vs DVOR — Key Swap
ReferenceVariableRotation

⚠ Exam Trap
DVOR swaps EVERYTHING: modulation types AND rotation direction. But the receiver can't tell the difference — works identically in the cockpit.

DVOR uses Doppler effect from ~50 antennas in a circle spinning at 30 rev/sec to create FM variable signal. FM is resistant to multipath → less scalloping.

ILS Signal Modulation

Both localiser and glideslope use two overlapping lobes on the SAME carrier frequency with different modulation frequencies:

90 Hz lobe Left of centreline / Above glidepath
150 Hz lobe Right of centreline / Below glidepath
DDM = 0 On centreline / On glidepath

DDM = Difference in Depth of Modulation. Receiver compares 90 Hz vs 150 Hz strength.

NDB / ADF

NON-DIRECTIONAL BEACON // AUTOMATIC DIRECTION FINDER
NDB Basics
Frequency 190 – 1750 kHz
Band LF / MF
Propagation Ground waves
ADF needle Always points at station
Station passage Needle flips 180°
NDB Errors — The Full List
  • Coastal refraction — signal bends at land/sea boundary toward coast. Worst at shallow crossing angles.
  • Mountain effect — signal diffracts (bends) around mountains. Long wavelength wraps around obstacles.
  • Night effect — sky wave interference at dawn/dusk. Ionospheric reflection contaminates ground wave.
  • Thunderstorm effect — ADF needle attracted toward CB/lightning static.
  • Quadrantal error — aircraft metal re-radiates signal, pulling needle toward nearest cardinal axis. Max error at 045°/135°/225°/315°. Fixed with compensation loop.
  • Station interference — two NDBs on similar frequencies.

Failed RMI — Stuck Rose

If the compass rose is stuck, the ADF needle still points at the station, but the reading is offset by the stuck heading value.

Relative Bearing = Indication − Stuck Heading
Example: indication 225° − stuck at 090° = 135° relative bearing
Refraction vs Diffraction

Refraction

Signal bends at a boundary between two mediums (land/sea). Like a shopping trolley going from tile to carpet.

Diffraction

Signal bends around an obstacle (mountain). Long wavelengths wrap around large objects.

NDB (long wavelength) → bends around/through things → refraction, diffraction
VOR (short wavelength) → bounces off things → reflection

VOR Errors
  • Site error / Scalloping — reflections from buildings/terrain near the station cause CDI to wobble. This is a VOR/ILS problem, NOT NDB.
  • Cone of confusion — unreliable signals directly overhead the station.
  • Terrain shielding — VHF is line-of-sight, mountains block signal.
⚠ Scalloping vs Beam Bend
Beam bend = steady offset in one direction.
Scalloping = oscillating needle swing side to side.
Both caused by reflections off terrain/buildings.
VOR Quadrant Questions

To find your quadrant with OBS set:

  • Line 1: OBS course and its reciprocal (e.g. 025° ↔ 205°)
  • Line 2: Perpendicular = add 90° to both (e.g. 115° ↔ 295°)
  • TO/FROM → which half of the station
  • Fly L/Fly R → which quarter

The perpendicular line = TO/FROM boundary. CDI has no heading input — heading is irrelevant.

Conversion Angle (GCT vs RLT)
CA = (Δ Longitude × sin Latitude) ÷ 2
Conversion angle between Great Circle & Rhumb Line

VOR radials = magnetic bearings at the station. Use station's variation, not aircraft's.

Northern Hemisphere: GC bulges north.
Going west → GCT > RLT → ADD CA
Going east → GCT < RLT → SUBTRACT CA

DME

DISTANCE MEASURING EQUIPMENT
DME Basics
Frequency 960 – 1215 MHz (UHF)
Measures Slant range (NOT ground distance)
Station delay 50 µs
Max capacity ~100 aircraft
Speed of radio 1 km = 3.33 µs
DME Distance Calculation
1. Total time − 50 µs (station delay)
2. ÷ 3.33 = distance in km
3. ÷ 1.852 = distance in NM
4. ÷ 2 = one-way slant range
Step-by-step DME calculation

Example: 2000 µs → 1950 → 586 km → 316 NM → 158 NM

DME Overload

When 100+ aircraft interrogate simultaneously, the station reduces sensitivity to respond only to the strongest (closest) signals. Faraway aircraft lose DME service first.

DME Arc — Speed Reads Zero

On a DME arc, distance from station stays constant. DME calculates groundspeed from rate of change of distance. No change = zero knots indicated, even though the aircraft is moving.

⚠ Exam Trap
DME always measures SLANT range, not ground distance. The difference matters when close to the station at high altitude.

False Glideslope (Side Lobes)

The GP antenna produces side lobes creating false glidepaths at odd multiples of the real GP (~6°, 9°, 12°). CDI shows centred with no flags — looks valid but you're way too high.

⚠ Intercept From Below
ALWAYS intercept the glideslope from BELOW. This ensures you capture the real 3° GP first, not a false one above it.

At 1 NM / 1200 ft = ~12° path = 4× the real GP = side lobe territory.

ILS Errors
  • Beam bend — steady CDI offset from reflections
  • Scalloping — CDI oscillates side to side from multiple reflections
  • False glideslope — side lobes above real GP

ILS sensitive area protects the signal from interference. For CAT II, course shift tolerance = 7.5 m (25 ft) from centreline. Vehicles entering this area cause reflections → monitoring system shuts down the signal.

MMR — Multi-Mode Receiver

One module that can process signals from ILS, MLS, or GNSS approaches. Processes one system at a time (not simultaneously). Main advantage: single module handles different landing systems depending on airport availability.

MLS Principle — Scanning Beams

MLS uses scanning beams, not fixed beams like ILS. The beam sweeps TO and FROM:

Aircraft position = time interval between TO and FROM scans

⚠ Exam Trap
ILS uses DDM (difference in depth of modulation). MLS uses TIME between scans. Don't confuse them — DDM in an MLS answer = wrong.
MLS Advantages over ILS
  • Higher frequency (SHF) → less ground reflections
  • Scanning beam can reject reflections (arrives at wrong time)
  • Works in hilly areas and near buildings
  • Curved/steep/offset approaches possible
  • Multiple glide slopes selectable by pilot
  • 200 channels (ILS has only 40)

CDI / RMI / HSI

COCKPIT NAVIGATION INSTRUMENTS
CDI (OBI) — Course Deviation Indicator
  • Deviation bar with 5 dots each side
  • OBS knob to select course
  • TO/FROM flag
  • NO heading information
  • Works with VOR and ILS localiser
CDI Radial Rule
Centre the bar with OBS:
FROM flag → TOP number = your radial
TO flag → BOTTOM number = your radial
RMI — Radio Magnetic Indicator
  • Compass rose rotates with heading
  • Green needle = ADF pointer (NDB)
  • Yellow needle = VOR pointer
  • Needle HEAD = QDM (bearing TO)
  • Needle TAIL = QDR (radial FROM)
  • No CDI, no dots, no deviation — just pointers
HSI — Horizontal Situation Indicator
  • CDI + Heading indicator merged into one
  • Compass rose shows actual heading
  • Course pointer rotates on compass rose
  • CDI bar sits inside course pointer
  • TO/FROM shown as arrow direction
  • Heading bug for desired heading
  • No reverse sensing — always intuitive
  • May include bearing pointers (like RMI needles)

PRIMARY RADAR

PSR // RADAR PRINCIPLES
Primary Radar Basics
Principle Pulse → reflect → receive echo
Measures Bearing + Range = 2D position
Bearing from Antenna direction at time of echo
Range from Round-trip travel time of pulse
Cannot measure Altitude or identity
MTI — Moving Target Indicator

Uses Doppler effect to compare successive returns. Stationary objects (ground, buildings) show no Doppler shift → removed from display. Moving objects (aircraft) show Doppler shift → kept on display.

Result: ground clutter eliminated, clean display showing only aircraft.

Main Lobe vs Side Lobes

Main Lobe

The intentional, strong, focused beam. This is the useful signal pointed where the antenna aims.

Side Lobes

Unintentional, weak energy leaked in wrong directions. Causes false glidepaths (ILS) and false radar targets.

Pulse Power

Pulse power = peak power of the pulse. During the pulse, the transmitter is at full blast — constant maximum power.

Average power = peak power × duty cycle. Much lower since the radar is OFF most of the time.

SSR Interrogation Pulses
P1 Main pulse (directional)
P2 (2 µs after P1) Sidelobe suppression (omni)

P1–P3 = 21 µs Mode C interrogation

P2 > P1 → aircraft in sidelobe → ignore. P1–P3 gap = mode. P4 length = all-call type. Transponder replies with squawk OR altitude per interrogation, never both.

Mode S — 24-Bit Address
  • Hard-coded into transponder — permanent, unique, like a MAC address
  • Cannot be changed from cockpit
  • Assigned by national aviation authority
  • Enables selective interrogation — radar talks to one aircraft only
  • Eliminates garbling from overlapping all-call replies
  • Long P4 tells acquired Mode S aircraft to stop replying to all-calls

Rule: always compensate for aircraft pitch to keep beam scanning the atmosphere, not the ground or sky above.

WX+T Mode (Modern AWR)

WX+T = Weather + Turbulence. Uses Doppler shift + pulse flattening to detect turbulence within precipitation.

Display colour Magenta
Range ~40 NM
Only works in Wet turbulence (needs precipitation)
⚠ Cannot Detect CAT
Needs precipitation particles to bounce off. Clear Air Turbulence = invisible to AWR by definition.
Cloud Top Formula
Cloud Top = (Tilt − ½ Beam Width) × 100 × Distance
Result = height above aircraft in feet. Add aircraft altitude for total.

Take reading when display becomes clear (blank) in the storm area — beam has passed above the top.

Example: (3.5° − 2.5°) × 100 × 120 NM = 12,000 ft above aircraft

AWR Gotchas
  • Polar areas — sea ice edge mistaken for real coastline in mapping mode
  • Resolution — improves as range decreases (beam covers smaller area). One blob can split into two echoes as you get closer.
  • Range scale — zooming in (reducing scale) makes returns bigger and move toward screen edge
  • Steep gradient — rapid colour change (green→red) over short distance = severe windshear
  • Ground mapping — tilt down, use identifiable features for position fix. Gives magnetic bearing + range.

Quick Rules
  • ILS = odd decimal, VOR = even decimal
  • Always intercept glideslope from BELOW
  • VOR radials use station's magnetic variation
  • Climb → tilt down, Descent → tilt up
  • CDI: FROM → top = radial, TO → bottom = radial
  • RMI: head = QDM, tail = radial
  • NDB errors: coastal, mountain, night, thunderstorm, quadrantal
  • VOR errors: site error, scalloping, cone of confusion
  • DME arc entry: sectors 1 & 3 only (no sector 2)
  • NDB hold without DME/GNSS: use TIME, not distance
  • Long wavelength = bends. Short wavelength = bounces.
  • Mode S = Small (25 ft). Mode C = Century (100 ft).
