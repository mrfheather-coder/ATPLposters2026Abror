Definitions
Core Concepts
CYCLE
One complete oscillation — full + and − peak sequence
PERIOD (τ)
Time to complete one cycle
measured in seconds / μs / ms
FREQUENCY (f)
Cycles per second
measured in Hz / kHz / MHz / GHz
WAVELENGTH (λ)
Distance wave travels during one complete cycle
measured in metres
Key Formulas
Frequency from Period
f = 1 / τ
Wavelength
λ = c / f
c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (speed of light)
Exam shortcut (f in MHz)
λ = 300 / f(MHz)
Answer in metres — fastest method!
Frequency from Wavelength
f = c / λ
Convert λ to metres first!

Polarization
VERTICAL
Wave oscillates up/down. Antenna vertical. NDB, VHF comms.
HORIZONTAL
Wave oscillates side to side. Antenna horizontal. VOR, ILS localiser.
CIRCULAR
Wave rotates like corkscrew. Helical antenna. GPS — works at any aircraft attitude.
⚠ GOLDEN RULE
TX and RX antennas MUST have same polarization — otherwise massive signal loss!

Ground Wave
Travels along Earth's surface via diffraction. Follows curvature of Earth. Long wavelengths only.
RANGE
VLF/LF — excellent. MF — moderate. HF — very short. VHF+ — nil.
SURFACE
Sea = high conductivity = long range. Dry sand = low conductivity = short range.
Sky Wave
Shoots upward, refracts through ionosphere, returns to ground. Long range.
DAY
Strong ionization — HF refracts back ✅. MF absorbed ❌.
NIGHT
Weak ionization — HF escapes to space ❌. MF refracts back ✅.
⚠ NOT REFLECTION!
Sky waves REFRACT through ionosphere — they don't reflect off it like a mirror.
Space Wave
Travels in straight line. Also called quasi-optical. Line of sight only. VHF and above.
WHY LOS?
No ionosphere bounce (punches through) + no ground wave (too short λ).
NAVAIDS
VOR, ILS, VHF comms, GPS — all line of sight.

HF Skip Zone

Ground wave dies out → sky wave hasn't landed yet → DEAD SPACE / SKIP ZONE

At Night:
Ionization ↓ → Skip distance ↑ → Bigger dead zone

Aircraft can fly INTO the skip zone after sunset — loses HF contact with distant ATC.

Signal Degradation
ATTENUATION
Signal weakens with range. Higher freq = more attenuation by weather.
SCATTERING
Wave hits droplets → energy spreads in random directions.
ABSORPTION
Wave energy converted to heat by atmosphere/ionosphere.
MULTIPATH
Same signal arrives from multiple directions via reflections. Causes bearing errors.
FADING
Destructive interference between sky + ground wave. Night effect on MF/HF.
Types of Modulation
AMPLITUDE (AM)
Height of wave changes. Frequency stays constant. Most common in aviation.
→ VHF comms, HF comms, VOR, ILS, ADF/NDB
FREQUENCY (FM)
Frequency changes. Amplitude stays constant. More resistant to interference.
→ Sound broadcasting (not aviation navaids)
PHASE (PM)
Phase of carrier wave reverses 180°. Very precise. Used in GNSS.
→ GPS, MLS
PULSE
Carrier switched ON/OFF rapidly. Short bursts, then silence for listening.
→ Radar, DME

Propagation Delay
Distance to target
D = (c × t) / 2
÷2 because round trip (there AND back)
Time from distance
t = (2 × D) / c
⚠ ALWAYS
Speed of LIGHT (c = 3×10⁸ m/s) — NEVER speed of sound!
Minimum Range
Minimum detection range
R_min = (c × PL) / 2
PL = Pulse Length (in seconds/μs)
Pulse length from min range
PL = (2 × R_min) / c
While transmitting, antenna is DEAF. Echoes from close targets arrive during TX pulse → missed! Shorter pulse = better close range.
Max Unambiguous Range
Maximum Unambiguous Range
MUR = c / (2 × PRF)
PRF from MUR
PRF = c / (2 × MUR)

PRF ↔ PRI Relationship
Same as f = 1/τ !
PRF = 1 / PRI    PRI = 1 / PRF
PRF = Pulse Repetition Frequency (Hz) | PRI = Pulse Repetition Interval (seconds)
PRI = the silence between pulses — listening time
PRF = how many pulses per second
Longer PRI → lower PRF → longer MUR

Golden Definitions
FADING
Interference of sky waves with ground waves
Only happens at night when both exist — MF/HF
MODULATION
Adding information onto a carrier wave during transmission
AM / FM / Phase / Pulse
DIFFRACTION
Radio wave bends over and around obstacles
Better at low freq (long λ). NDB diffracts, VOR doesn't
ABSORPTION
Radio wave energy taken up by atmosphere
MF sky wave absorbed by ionosphere during daytime
ATTENUATION
Signal loses strength as range increases
Higher freq = more attenuation by weather/rain
INTERFERENCE
Superimposition of two waves at same/similar frequency
Constructive = stronger | Destructive = weaker/cancels
REFLECTION
Wave bounces off boundary without passing through
Primary radar, multipath off buildings
REFRACTION
Wave changes direction due to change in speed/medium
HF sky waves in ionosphere — NOT reflection!
MULTIPATH
Same signal arrives from multiple directions via reflections off structures
Causes bearing errors in VOR, ILS, GPS
SUPERPOSITION
When two or more waves meet, they add together at that point
The principle that causes interference/fading
